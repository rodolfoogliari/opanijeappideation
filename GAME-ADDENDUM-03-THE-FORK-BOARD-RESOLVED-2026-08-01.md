## 3 · Fork J, deferred — and the criterion is worth more than the answer

**What was asked.** Should the reference part ever drop out so the student hears for himself whether
he held.

**What was answered.** Not by design, and not now. **Only where the music itself calls for it** —
and probably not in the first toques.

**Why this is a better answer than any of the three options offered.** All three options treated the
dropout as a *design* question: ours to add, demote, or test. The founder's answer moves it out of
the design space entirely and makes it a property of the material. If a toque contains a place where
the drums drop, the app renders it because it is in the music. If it does not, the app does not
invent one. That is the same logic that put the reference pulse on an instrument of the tradition
rather than on a metronome (R15), and it needs no governance construction to hold — there is nothing
for the app to fire.

**What follows immediately.**

- **R76's rough recording halves.** Takes 3 and 4 were the two dropouts. They leave the list. The
  recording goes from six takes to four (§7).
- **Fork C's weight goes up sharply.** With dropouts out, breaks and turnarounds are the **only**
  additive musical event the game has. Fork C stops being an enhancement. See §5.
- **Risk #25 is unchanged and still true.** Under scheduled audio (D60) the part sounds correct
  regardless, so nothing in Release 1 tells a student he has not got it. The dropout was the one
  mechanism that would have, and it is now out. This is accepted, not overlooked, and it is the
  strongest argument for revisiting Fork I at M1.

**What stays open.** If a toque *does* carry a musically-native dropout, rendering it is still a red
line #1 form decision — Junior's, said in words, not inferred from a take. Recorded so it is not
discovered at the shoot.

## 4 · Fork B, closed — and the screen drum stops being "advanced"

**The ruling (G13, E20).** The first session runs the whole instrument: voice → voice over the
battery → battery → the tap door → the screen drum, all of it, at its simplest setting.

**This satisfies FF3 cleanly**, and it is the first ruling in this conversation that does. FF3's
acceptance test is single-player adults *and* beginners who cannot play to a metronome, **both able
to play on day one**. Option 1 (session ends at the door) arguably breached it for someone who
already owns sticks. This does not.

**But it revises R70, and the revision should be visible.** R70 recommended the screen sits *behind*
the basic door — "never as a shortcut past the instrument." Under R74 the tap door and the screen
drum are one instrument at different zone counts, and under G13 a beginner reaches the drum room on
day one. **The order R70 asked for survives; the distance does not.** "Advanced mode" is no longer
advanced. It is the product.

**Four consequences, stated now.**

1. **Risk #23 rises and should be re-read.** A successful phone game whose users never buy an
   instrument, never book a class and never go to Salvador inverts the funnel the company is built
   on. R70's sequencing was the mitigation. Compression is not the same as removal — the order still
   runs voice first, and E10's Salvador invitation still sits at the ending — but the mitigation is
   weaker than it was this morning. **Mitigation now rests on E19's instrument question and on E10,
   not on distance.**
2. **INPUT-69's urgency rises.** Junior's form assent on a glass surface standing in for a drum was
   a question about a mode most students would reach late. It is now a question about **the free
   first session**. It stays gathered with INPUT-41, INPUT-52 and INPUT-62 in one mockup
   conversation, but it is no longer the least urgent of the four.
3. **Mandate 01 §10's tenth item now gates the free door.** "No screen-drum surface shipped without
   Junior having seen it" was written about advanced mode. It now blocks Release 1's first session.
4. **The stroke sample library moves onto the day-one path** — if it survives C22. See §8.

**Ledger row 46 (new).** *A beginner can be carried from voice to the screen drum in one sitting* —
HYPOTHESIS. Converts at M1 on first-session completion, or earlier and more cheaply through the
seven-question usability pilot in the research review §9.3.

## 7 · The count-in, and a reward object that just became furniture

**The ruling (G16).** Junior's count-in fires at **every session start**, and the variant bank is
shot.

**What this gets right.** It is the strongest reward class in the evidence base — a real human voice,
his, uncloneable, already being filmed — and firing it at every start makes the app open the way the
room opens. The master calls you in. That is truer to the material than firing it as a prize for
advancing.

**What it costs, and it is not obvious.** **A thing that happens every time is not a reward. It is
furniture.** R60(b) named the count-in as one of Release 1's three mechanics *because* it was the
celebration object. Reassigning it to the opening is very likely correct — but it leaves the
celebration slot empty, and Mandate 01 §4.4 forbids a synthetic substitute, so it cannot be filled
cheaply later. **Fork D therefore half-closes and half-stays-open. INPUT-73.**

**And it makes the variant bank load-bearing rather than mitigating.** R72 was written against a
count-in firing on advance — a few times a week. At every start it fires daily. A bank of three is
a jingle inside a fortnight, and the wear is unrecoverable because a synthetic substitute is barred.

**R79 (new, pre-shoot, strong).** Shoot **ten to twelve** count-in variants, not three to five.
Include at least two that are not interchangeable with the rest — a longer one, and one that reads as
a return after absence (R66's musical streak repair has no asset behind it otherwise). The marginal
cost is minutes of a day that is already set up; the cost of being short is a permanent one.
**INPUT-74 puts the number to the founder rather than assuming it.**

## 8 · C22 — does the stroke sample library survive D60?

**The contradiction.** D58 put the stroke sample library on the M0 pre-shoot list. Its justification
is Game Addendum 01 §6.4: *"the screen produces no sound when struck, so the app must."* **D60
replaced that architecture.** Under scheduled playback the app plays Junior's correct part in time
from the render and the strike keeps it alive; it does not fire a sample per strike. The
justification D58 was written on no longer holds.

**This is not an argument for dropping it.** Three things plausibly still need one-shots, and none of
them is settled:

- **Off-grid and wrong-zone strikes.** Under D59 a strike inside the window lands in the groove. What
  a strike *outside* the window sounds like is not specified anywhere in the estate. If the answer is
  "nothing," the instrument is silent when you are wrong, which is a design position and should be
  taken deliberately. If the answer is any sound at all, it needs samples.
- **Any surface that is not playing the render** — a zone-learning screen, a first-touch moment
  before the battery starts.
- **The capture is irrecoverable and the questions are not.** Twenty to forty minutes at the shoot
  buys the option; not shooting it forecloses answers we have not reached yet.

**R80 (new).** **Keep D58 in scope.** Record that its justification changed, so nobody later finds
§6.4's reasoning, checks it against D60, and quietly cuts a shoot item on a superseded premise. The
technical half is build-side; the shoot-day half is a founder decision and is **INPUT-76**.

**INPUT-70 is unaffected and still Junior's** — whether any stroke or drum is off-limits to isolated
sampling under the partition. G14 names the strokes we want; it does not license taking them.

## 9 · The stroke set, and one distinction that must not be lost

**The ruling (G14).** Three zones on hand instruments — **slap, tone, bass**. Two on stick
instruments — **rim and skin**.

**This is the zone *count* and the zone *stroke*. It is not the zone *label*.** D53 removed Western
notation product-wide and D56 states that the screen drum's zones are **named by the syllables**.
"Slap / tone / bass" are generic percussion terms in English; they are how the founder specified
which strokes exist, and they are the right specification. **What the student sees on the zone is
Junior's syllable, not the English word.** Stated here because the two are easy to collapse and the
collapse would reintroduce exactly the notation D53 removed.

**What this closes.** The dial's stops are now defined by the material rather than by a designer's
choice of difficulty steps — three zones and two zones, not "easy/medium/hard." Under R74 the dial
runs from the tap door's single zone up to the instrument's full stroke set, and G14 fixes where the
top of that dial is.

**What it opens. INPUT-77** — which instruments in the free rhythm's battery take the three-zone hand
vocabulary and which take the two-zone stick vocabulary, and whether the reference instrument (R15)
needs a vocabulary of its own. **The founder names the mapping; Junior confirms it against the
solfejo**, since under G3 the syllable set is what actually defines the stroke set and a mismatch
there would show up as a zone with no syllable or a syllable with no zone.

## 10 · Fork H deferred — and what it costs at M1

**The ruling (G15).** No engagement mechanics on the free tier. Decision deferred, nothing given
for now.

**This is the only reversible direction and the founder took it.** Red line #5 makes anything given
to the free tier permanent, **including mechanics**. Giving nothing now and adding later is
available; giving now and withdrawing later is not. Mandate 01's INPUT-66 recommended the opposite,
and the recommendation was made without weighing red line #5's ratchet against the value of the
measurement. **The founder's answer is better than the recommendation and the recommendation is
withdrawn.**

**But it has a price and the price is the measurement.** M1's two headline exit numbers —
first-session completion and day-7 return — are taken at the free door. If no mechanic runs there,
**M1 measures a product without an engagement layer.** Ledger row 38 (*the engagement layer lifts
day-7 return and first-session completion*) had no counterfactual to begin with because Release 1
ships once; it now has no *treatment* either. **Risk #26.**

**R81 (new).** Say so in advance. Record M1's two numbers explicitly as a **no-engagement-layer
baseline** in the exit evidence, dated and labelled, before the numbers exist. Otherwise the first
real number this company produces gets read later as a verdict on a toolkit that was never switched
on. Per-mechanic instrumentation (D50) still gets built; it simply has nothing to report at the free
door in Release 1, and that is a finding rather than a gap.

**INPUT-62's urgency falls** — Junior's form assent on the engagement layer over his free rhythm is
now a question about a layer that does not run there. It stays in the mockup conversation, and it
should be asked about whatever *does* run past the door rather than dropped.

## 11 · Fork K (new) — what pulls the student back on day 7

**Opened by this session's own decisions, and named rather than left to be discovered.**

The estate identified the **return problem** as one of two structural gaps: nothing pulling a
wavering beginner back between monthly master cycles. Three of the instruments that might have
addressed it were switched off today, each for a good reason:

- comparison between students — out (G17)
- share and export — out (G18)
- the engagement layer at the free door — off (G15)

**What is left for day-7 return in Release 1:** notification craft (D45), the implementation
intention at onboarding (R67 — the largest well-supported effect in the research base, d = 0.65, at
the cost of one screen), the room map filling in (R65), the count-in as the opening ritual (G16),
Junior's monthly listening cycle, and the intrinsic pull of the groove itself, which the evidence
rates STRONG and which this product has more of than the apps the research was done on.

**That may well be enough** — and if it is, it is the strongest possible vindication of the thesis
that intrinsic motivation outperforms extrinsic mechanics here. **It should be argued, not assumed.**
Fork K is the argument. It is a mockup-stage fork, not a pre-shoot one, and it is not worked in this
file.
