# Opanijé — Contradiction Register (C1–C23)

**Status.** Register of record for this series as of 2026-08-03, under D83 (Addendum 04 §7).
**Supersedes.** The per-session tables in `sessions/` for this series, which remain readable as
derivation history but are no longer the register.
**Range.** C1 through C23.
**Rule.** Rows are appended, never rewritten. A row's status may change; its claim may not.

**What a contradiction is.** A place where two estate documents say incompatible things. The estate
does not delete the losing statement — it records which one governs and why. Every contradiction
therefore carries a **disposition**, and the disposition is the thing of record.

**Contradictions are always C + a number** (C1 … C22). They are unrelated to the forks, which are
bare letters — **Fork C is a fork, not C3** — and to the founder rulings, which are A + number. See
`NUMBERING.md`.

**Where the series comes from.** C1–C20 are v2.0 §17, "Contradiction disposition (C1–C20)".
Addendum 02 §11 opens **C21**. Addendum 03 §8 works **C22**.

**The most recent disposition is C22's.** Addendum 04 §3.3 records **D77 (ADOPTED; resolves C22)** —
*the drum always sounds*: in-window strikes keep Junior's correct part alive (G10 unchanged),
out-of-window and wrong-zone strikes fire a one-shot of the real stroke, because a real drum struck
off-time still sounds. That restates D58's justification on solid ground and **re-justifies the
stroke sample library** that D60 had left without a reason. The library stays in the M0 shoot.

**Door labels.** Under D83 item 2 every OPEN item carries one. **No row in this series is open** — all
twenty-two are disposed — so the table carries none. Where a disposition still hangs on an item that
is open elsewhere in the estate, that item and its door are named in *Dispositions that still hang on
an open item*, below.
