## 2 · Deliverable spec, per capture row

**Filename convention.** Every file the day produces is named at ingest, never left as
`DSC_0001`:

```
OPJ_<BLOCK>-<ITEM>_<PART>_<TEMPO>_tk<NN>_<YYYYMMDD>.<ext>
```

- `<BLOCK>` — the run-of-show letter (C, D, E, F, G, H, J, K above).
- `<ITEM>` — the instrument, rhythm or asset: `RUM`, `RUMPI`, `LE`, `AGOGO`; `IJEXA`, `CONGO`,
  `CABILA`; `WELCOME`, `INVITE-DOM`, `INVITE-SSA`, `COUNTIN-01`…`COUNTIN-12`, `COUNTIN-RETURN`.
- `<PART>` / `<TEMPO>` — where applicable: part name and `SLOW` / `NORM` (the speed set follows
  INPUT-59; label with what was actually played).
- `tk<NN>` — take number, two digits, never reused within an item.
- Stroke one-shots extend `<ITEM>` with stroke and velocity: `F-RUM-SLAP-V3_tk02`.
- The consent recording itself: `OPJ_A-CONSENT_<YYYYMMDD>` — first file of the day, by rule.

**Slate discipline.** Every take opens with a spoken slate — item id, take number, date — read by
the operator, not by Junior (R53's bar on the master's voice naming products argues for keeping his
takes clean of production talk). For stems: the slate also names the part and which parts are
already in his headphones, because that is the fact an editor cannot reconstruct.

## 3 · Ingest and backup

**The rule of the day, verbatim from the runbook:** *"Back up every file to two locations before
dinner. Not tomorrow morning."* (`plans/PLAN-02-RUNBOOK.md` Day 8; restated
`plans/PLAN-01-BUILD.md:179`.)

1. **Dual copy, on the day.** Two physically separate targets — working disk plus an off-machine
   copy. No card is wiped until both copies exist and step 2 has passed on both.
2. **Checksum step.** `sha256sum` manifest generated at first ingest, verified on the second copy.
   sha256 deliberately: it is the same primitive as the app's audio-provenance chain
   (`plans/PLAN-01-BUILD.md:144-145`), so the day's manifest feeds Day 9's pipeline without
   re-hashing.
3. **The escrow gate — answered before the shoot, and not by this file.** `plan/ROADMAP.md:16-30`
   (§0, "escrow, before anything else"): the estate is one machine holding the code, every
   credential, the backup key and the signing key — *"Losing it after M0 means losing the company's
   only irreplaceable asset."* **INPUT-44** is the founder's standing ruling on whether "no second
   machine" survives contact with these recordings (`registers/INPUTS.md:71` — OPEN;
   `plan/M0-SHOOT.md:163` — "blocks M0 asset delivery"). D98's deferral (`BUILD-LOG.md:92-96`,
   risk #37) keeps the single-machine total-loss exposure open. **This pack does not invent the
   answer.** It records the gate: ⟦OPERATOR⟧ rules INPUT-44 before the day, and the second copy in
   step 1 lands wherever that ruling says. A shoot day executed with the gate unanswered puts the
   only irreplaceable asset on the exposure the ruling exists to close.

## 4 · The in-room decision card

Two decisions can only be taken on the day, with Junior in the room. Written to be **read aloud** by
the operator, in plain language, both consequences stated — per the estate's own format
(`METHOD.md` §6: options with pros and cons, plainer when asked). Junior's answers are written down
in his words (Block I).

**Card 1 — INPUT-23, isolated per-instrument stems** (`plan/M0-SHOOT.md:51,161`;
`registers/INPUTS.md:50` — OPEN).

> "We would like to record each drum's part **separately**, one at a time, layered over your own
> playing in headphones — not only the full battery together. This cannot be done later: isolated
> parts cannot be extracted from a performance; they exist only if we record them today
> (impossible without re-recording — v2.0 §7.2 rank 3).
>
> **If yes:** the app can teach by *subtraction* — parts drop away one at a time, and the student's
> place in the music opens up. The subtraction ladder is rendered **from these stems**
> (v2.0 §7.2 rank 3; `plan/M0-SHOOT.md:51`).
>
> **If no:** we record the battery whole, and that ladder does not exist in this product — not
> delayed, absent, because it cannot be built from a mixed recording. **A no changes the product.**
> Both answers are honoured; only one is recoverable."

**Card 2 — INPUT-70, isolated one-shot samples** (`plan/M0-SHOOT.md:54,135`;
`registers/INPUTS.md:97` — OPEN).

> "Separately from recording your *music*, we would like short isolated samples — single strokes,
> one at a time, on each instrument. These fire inside the app when a student strikes outside the
> window: the drum always sounds (D77).
>
> **This is a different question from the music, and we are asking it as one.** A single isolated
> stroke of a drum that may be consecrated is a **different object** from a passage of music played
> on it — `plan/M0-SHOOT.md:135`: *"G14 names the strokes wanted; it does not license taking
> them."* The consent instrument names one-shot sampling as a licensed **class**
> (`registers/INPUTS.md:116`, INPUT-89); **which drums, which strokes, and whether any drum is
> off-limits to isolated sampling under the partition is yours, drum by drum, here.** This card is
> the cross-link the registers do not yet carry: the instrument question (INPUT-89) and the
> per-drum ruling (INPUT-70) are the same consent surface at two depths, and both must be answered
> — the paper does not settle the drum, and the drum does not settle the paper.
>
> **If a drum is off-limits:** its strokes are not sampled; out-of-window strikes on that
> instrument fall back to a permitted instrument's samples — a build-side accommodation, not a
> product change.
> **If all are permitted:** the full library per G14/D65 is captured in Block F."

## 6 · Consent-execution checklist

Red line #6 in operating form, verbatim (`CHARTER.md:121-124`): **"Any recording without an executed
consent instrument does not ship. No exceptions."** Consent is captured **before the cameras roll**,
and every consent instrument is recorded **in the teller's own voice** (`CHARTER.md:117-119`).

**Before the day:**

- [ ] Counsel's draft in hand — INPUT-89's operative form: interactive and game use, isolated stems
      **and** one-shot sampling in scope, broad the first time (R19), bilingual
      (`registers/INPUTS.md:116`; `plans/PLAN-01-BUILD.md:155-157`). Scope ratified ALL-AGREED
      2026-08-14, threading in flight — the registers still carry INPUT-22/-89 OPEN.
- [ ] **The wording names VOCALIZATIONS explicitly as a licensed object distinct from speech.** The
      2026-08-14 ratification covered every use tier; **the wording gap is what survives it.** The
      partition's second value — *gamifiable, recognition/vocalization only* (`CHARTER.md:66-79`) —
      makes vocalization a load-bearing product surface; an instrument that licenses "voice" or
      "speech" without naming vocalizations leaves the estate's most-used tier resting on
      interpretation. ⟦OPERATOR⟧ confirms the drafted clause with counsel before printing.
- [ ] Two printed copies (`plans/PLAN-02-RUNBOOK.md` Day 7).
- [ ] INPUT-32 — Junior's ruling on marking the name — taken before and independent of legality
      (`plan/M0-SHOOT.md:50,162`). A ruling, not a capture; it does not gate the day but the day is
      the cheap moment to have it.
- [ ] INPUT-44 ruled (§3) and INPUT-87 (authoritative arrangement) settled
      (`plans/PLAN-00-DECISIONS.md:319`), so the day's output has somewhere to land.

**On the day, in order, before anything rolls:**

- [ ] The instrument read aloud, in Portuguese, plainly — questions honoured before signature.
- [ ] Signed on paper. Both copies.
- [ ] **Consent recorded in Junior's own voice** — first file of the day
      (`OPJ_A-CONSENT_<YYYYMMDD>`, §2), stating in his words what he is licensing, including
      vocalizations, stems and one-shots by name.
- [ ] Only then: Block B. **Cameras that rolled before this checklist completed produced material
      that does not ship** — that is the red line, not a schedule slip.
