## 1 · What changed, and why

Until 2026-08-03 the estate's posture on publication was **defensive**. v2.0 §11.5 published one
thing only — the base mechanic, as prior art, to stop somebody else patenting it — and drew a hard
non-publication line around everything else. Addendum 01 §2 then argued that the notation being
inseparable from a recording of a specific man made that non-publication discipline *materially
easier to hold*.

G22 moves publication from defensive to **strategic**. The notation system is introduced openly, as
a standard for teaching Afro-Brazilian percussion, and Opanijé positions as a music-education
company. D82 opens the workstream that carries it.

The same ruling reverses a critique the refactor had made of the product's own shape. Addendum 04
§1.2 records it plainly: the "free screen drum is value leakage" critique is **reversed** under G22
— the free instrument plus commons rhythms is the standard's **distribution vehicle**, and giving it
away is the strategy, not the leak. The moat argument moves accordingly.

## 2 · The frame is published; the corpus is not

The distinction is the whole workstream. **What is published is the frame — the syllable system's
structure, presented as a standard.** What is never published is the material: the recordings, the
stems and the renders, which remain the priced scarcity (Addendum 04 §6).

This does not repeal v2.0 §11.5's non-publication rule so much as re-cut it. §11.5's rule ran
"publish the mechanic; never publish the catalog, Junior's partition, the stem architecture, or the
pedagogical sequence — the clone does not need the mechanic, he needs the recordings, and those the
rights architecture withholds." D82 adds a second publishable object, the notation frame, and leaves
the corpus exactly where §11.5 left it.

**One boundary is genuinely undrawn.** Addendum 01 §2 established that in this product the notation,
the teaching and the input are *the same object* — one system does all three. §11.5's never-publish
list contains "the pedagogical sequence." The estate nowhere states where the syllable system's
publishable *structure* ends and the unpublishable *pedagogical sequence* begins. That line is
INPUT-83's first practical job and is recorded here as an open scoping question rather than
answered.

## 3 · The reference dialect — INPUT-67 reframed, not closed

INPUT-67 began as a form question under Mandate 01 §4.3: *does the app fix one syllable set as its
standard?* Addendum 01 §2 narrowed it to its residue — G3 had already settled that Junior's solfejo
encodes stroke as well as rhythm — and left the sharp part standing: whatever is recorded becomes
the transmission standard for everyone who learns here, permanently.

**Addendum 04 §6 reframes it rather than closing it.** The app fixes **Junior's syllable set as the
reference dialect** for the material he teaches, *inside a frame that lets other masters and other
traditions carry their own*. That is how living standards for oral material work — a standard for
speech does not abolish accents; it names one form as the reference against which others are read,
and remains legible when they differ. And it is the mechanism by which **red line #1 scales**: each
future bench's named authority governs its own tradition's syllables under the same frame, so
growing the catalog never requires imposing one master's syllables on another's tradition. Without
the reframe, the standard would have had to choose between staying one man's dialect forever and
overwriting the next master's — and red line #1 forbids the second.

**The reframe does not discharge the input.** The surviving half — Junior's own confirmation that
his set is fixed as the reference dialect — is still owed, and it is owed before the shoot, because
the shoot is what fixes it.

## 4 · FF1 flips valence

**FF1 is a FOUNDER-FACT and it names the clone risk.** Carlinhos Brown owns Timbalada and is
intimate with candomblé percussionists; if lineage or provenance were the source of value he holds
more of it socially than Opanijé and could reproduce a register at will. A provenance register is
the one asset a better-connected incumbent does not need to build. FF1, with A14, is what removed
provenance from the moat argument in the first place, and it is what put the adjacent incumbent on
the risk register as #11 — named, specific, local.

**Under G22 the sign changes.** An incumbent adopting a standard that Opanijé publishes *and
stewards* strengthens the position rather than eroding it: adoption spreads the frame, and the frame
points at its steward. The clause matters more than the claim — **provided stewardship is designed.**
An adopted standard with no steward is a gift, not a position.

**Stewardship is not designed today.** The estate names the requirement in one clause of Addendum 04
§6 and routes the design into INPUT-83's mitigation for risk #28. Nothing in the estate states who
stewards, by what instrument, with what governance, or how a dialect other than Junior's is
admitted. That is the workstream's largest open item and it is recorded as such.

## 7 · Sequencing

Nothing publishes before INPUT-83 returns. What can proceed now is everything that does not disclose
— and the sequencing constraint that actually bites is not the standard's own: it is the **GUI
industrial-design filing**, which v2.0 §11.4 marks time-critical **before any screens go public**,
and which matters now because a mockup is being built.

**The distinction to keep deliberate.** A mockup shown to Junior is not public; a mockup shown in a
pitch, a store listing, a press item or an adopter's hands is. The estate's build order assumes
lock-and-file (R49) and the ruling that settles it is INPUT-51, which is live rather than
theoretical for exactly this reason.

**And one derived constraint the estate has not written down.** Publishing a standard *under the
name* is further public use of the name. v2.0 §11.3 RULES the sequence R35 → R34 → any filing or
further public use. If the standard carries the name Opanijé, Junior's ruling (INPUT-32) and
clearance (INPUT-31) precede publication as a matter of the estate's own ruling, not of preference.
If the standard is published unnamed or under a separate mark, R43's house-mark / product-mark
separation is the instrument already held ready. This file records the constraint; it does not
choose between the two.
