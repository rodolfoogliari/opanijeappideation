### 0.1 · Epistemic labels (ten, plus one status)

- **RULED** — a founder or governance decision; needs no market validation.
- **REPORTED** — an asset asserted as existing; not independently audited.
- **ASSUMPTION** — a modeled number awaiting measurement.
- **HYPOTHESIS** — a causal claim that must be tested.
- **MEASURED** — supported by completed transactions or observed cohorts. *As of v2.0, nothing

  asserted in session and treated as true (FF1–FF4, §3.1).

  counsel confirmation before anything rests on it.

  carrying its own re-verification requirement (§19).

## 2 · The one law and the red lines (RULED — charter-level, carried unchanged)

The one law: **access is bought; standing is only earned.** Courses, memberships, classes and
seasons are for sale. Entries in the book, passage between stages, and a master's acknowledgment
can never be purchased.

The six red lines, to be written into the corporate documents before any first check clears:

1. **Each tradition's sacred boundary is governed by that tradition's own named authority.** For
   Ketu Candomblé material: Junior, always. No game mechanics on sacred material unless its
   authority designs the form himself; no sacred iconography in product art without approval.
2. **Standing is only earned.** No entry, passage, honour, or acknowledgment is ever purchasable.
3. **Supply is curated.** Masters enter by vouched invitation through a bench; no self-serve door.
4. **No synthetic voice, ever.** Every voice is a real recorded human; no cloned masters, no
   machine-composed calls "in his style."
5. **The free tier's permanent-access promise holds.** The house never takes back what it gave —
   and this attaches to anything the app gives away, including the Room's free set (R24/R30).
6. **Consent and credit are structural.** A teller's withdrawal takes his material down; credit is
   permanent; price worlds never cross-wire.

An investor unwilling to sign these is buying a different company, and the answer to that investor
is no. A milestone missed is a milestone missed; a red line crossed is the company ended.

**One red line has no operating form yet.** Red line #6 requires that a withdrawal takes material
down, and as of 2026-07-30 there is no cross-system takedown anywhere in the estate
(VERIFIED-INTERNAL; ledger row 30, FALSIFIED). **This is not a violation** — no consented material
has been published, so nothing has been promised that cannot be honoured. It is a hard requirement
before Release 1 ships any consented material, and it is written into Release 1 scope as the red
line's operating form rather than as a feature (§4.1). Whether the promise must reach copies
already on devices is the founder's and counsel's interpretive question (INPUT-47), and under D27
it does not gate Release 1.

## 3 · The product spine: the Room

### 3.1 · The four founder facts that shaped it (FOUNDER-FACT, treated as true)

- **FF1 — the clone risk has a name.** Carlinhos Brown owns Timbalada and is intimate with
  candomblé percussionists. If lineage/provenance were the source of value, he holds more of it
  socially than Opanijé and could reproduce a register at will. A provenance register is the one
  asset a better-connected incumbent does not need to build.
- **FF2 — activity versus record.** A record of past events does not create a weekly habit.
  Capoeira's corda marks progress *in* the game; the game is why people show up.
- **FF3 — the two groups any base product must serve:** players who want a single-player
  experience and will not interact with other users; and beginners who cannot play to a metronome
  — which is most beginners. Both must be able to play on day one. This is the acceptance test.
- **FF4 — "Opanijé" is a traditional rhythm:** the *toque* associated with Obaluaiê/Omolu in Ketu
  Candomblé. Consequences at §11.3.

FF2 moved the spine from register to game; FF3 moved the game to single-player-and-beginner-first;
FF1 and A14 (RULED: the differentiator is Opanijé's own gamified product, not a lineage or
provenance register) removed provenance from the moat argument. Percussion carries no recognized
lineage currency (Session 01 closing input); candomblé does — which is why the register could
never be the platform spine.

### 3.2 · The Room (base tier — ADOPTED; ratification INPUT-24)

**The core loop: the student plays their own part inside the master's actual recorded battery.**
With per-instrument isolated stems, Junior's or Vanderson's battery becomes a room the student can
be *inside* — alone, daily, the other parts played by real named musicians. It is the closest a
screen gets to what the immersion sells physically, and it is unobtainable by anyone without these
recordings under this rights architecture. **Stems are not a feature; they are the product's
existence condition** (R16/R31 — hence M0 rank 3 by irrecoverability, §7.2).

**How the Room is delivered at Release 1 — the subtraction ladder (ADOPTED; INPUT-41 open).**

The audit is unambiguous: the app has **no audio layer at all** — no audio library in the
dependency tree, one opaque media reference per lesson, no microphone capability, no filesystem
layer (VERIFIED-INTERNAL 2026-07-30). Sample-accurate multi-track playback cannot be reached by
extending it; independent player instances hold independent clocks and drift audibly within
seconds. Real multi-track needs a native engine on both platforms — `AVAudioEngine`, AAudio/Oboe,
or a shared C++ mixing core — plus a schema change at every layer so a catalog item can own a
*set* of files. Nobody in the estate has built mobile audio (risk #18).

The resolution is pedagogical, not technical. §3.2's subtraction is a *sequence*: agogô plus the
student's own part, then parts added back as they hold. **If that sequence is a ladder of fixed
levels rather than free part-by-part muting, every level can be pre-rendered server-side as a
single stereo file — and single files play on the player that already exists.**

| | Native multi-track engine | Pre-rendered ladder |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering | Specialist, from zero, both platforms | None — existing player |
| Live mute/unmute mid-play | Yes | No — a level change is a transition |
| Continuous tempo | Yes (with transient-preserving DSP) | No — fixed steps only |
| Audio quality | Risk of transient smear on percussion | Rendered offline, best possible |
| Storage | One stem set per item | Multiplied per level × tempo |
| Release 1 feasible | No | Yes |

Order-of-magnitude storage (ASSUMPTION): 4 parts → 4 student-part choices × 4 ladder levels × ~4
tempo steps ≈ 64 files per rhythm; at ~3 minutes stereo AAC ≈ 2.5–3 MB each ≈ ~180 MB per rhythm;
a ten-rhythm Release 1 catalog ≈ 1.5–2.5 GB. Trivial on object storage; **impossible on the
current 38 GB box at 73% full** (§4.8). Media moves to object storage and a CDN regardless of
which audio path is chosen (INPUT-48).

**Three things this does not do.** It does not defer the stems — they are shot at M0, they stay
rank 3, they remain impossible to obtain later, and the ladder is rendered from them. It does not
foreclose the native engine — if the ladder proves pedagogically inadequate, the engine is built
in Release 2 against material already in the can. And it does not decide anything reserved to the
masters: **whether a fixed ladder is honest teaching is Junior's and Vanderson's call (INPUT-41),
not engineering's, and they are in the room at the M0 shoot anyway.** The founder has relayed that
Junior considers slowing a toque normal, which closes the tempo half; the ladder half is open.

**If INPUT-41 forbids the ladder,** M1 slips by the length of a specialist audio engagement, that
engagement is priced against the free treasury and the R$5k floor (INPUT-43), and the schedule
consequence is stated in §7.4 rather than absorbed.

**The rest of the Room, carried from v1.0:**

- **Beginners are served by subtraction, not simplification.** Start with the agogô plus the
  student's own part; add parts back as they hold. The true on-ramp needs no instrument: palmas
  and the sung part. Session 02's tiers 1 (recognition) and 2 (vocalization) survive as the
  on-ramp *inside* one room, not as separate products. (Part-subtraction pedagogy is
  PM-ASSERTION → INPUT-30; vocalization-as-the-tradition's-teaching-method and the
  secular/liturgical continuum are PM-ASSERTION → INPUT-36.)
- **Who pulls the lever.** §3.2 says parts are added back "as they hold." **If the app decides they
  hold, that is a machine verdict and it breaks §3.3 through the pedagogy rather than through the
  scoring design.** Ruled by construction in this version: **the student pulls the lever.** The
  ladder advances on the student's own choice, never on the app's assessment.
- **The reference pulse is an instrument of the tradition, never a metronome** (R15 — ADOPTED).
- **One catalog with a per-item treatment field**, not two tradition-scoped products (R17 —
  ADOPTED, subject to INPUT-36). A13 (RULED): the catalog is not secular-only; candomblé rhythms
  circulate secularly in Brazil and are in scope as game material.
- **Tier 4 (peer/ensemble play — ensemble stacking, entrada/steal, call-and-response) is kept and
  is not the base** (A15 — RULED). If ever built, it is turn-based, because latency and codecs
  destroy synchronous musical interaction (R10), and asynchrony is what lets a Bahian and a
  Berliner play at all (A3 requires both audiences).

### 3.3 · Nothing scores the player (R28 — ADOPTED; this is R1 applied unchanged)

Two-layer architecture, carried:

- **Layer 1 — facts about the user's own play.** Minutes, cycles held, parts attempted, levels
  reached, their take beside the master's *(this last arrives with Release 2 — D26 removes the
  microphone from Release 1)*. Uncapped, automatable, safe: software may count facts. Layer 1 makes
  a student visible; **it never entitles.**
- **Layer 2 — human attestation.** A master's judgment. Capped by his hours, never computed.
  Judgment is human, monthly, public, by name: the cycle's response (§4.3).

**"Cycles held" is defined, because it read two ways and the fork was expensive.** In Release 1 it
means *the student stayed in the loop for N cycles* — a playback fact, counted without listening.
It does not mean the app heard them keep the pattern. Whether the app ever listens at all is
R45/INPUT-49, and under D26 no microphone ships in Release 1, so the question is settled for M1 by
construction and open for Release 2.

Consequences: no machine verdicts anywhere (red-line-safe by construction); INPUT-17 stops being
load-bearing (private self-facing feedback is deferred — Junior's call if ever proposed); peer
voting and its harassment surface never arise; audio latency stops blocking anything, because
latency binds scoring, not playing along; and retention runs on an appointment with a named human
who might say your name, not on a streak. (Reading of A14 this rests on — a proprietary
interactive product, not a points layer — is INPUT-28, the one root reading that could be wrong.)

### 3.4 · The Caderno, demoted and clarified (R18 — ADOPTED; resolves C4, C6)

The Caderno is **what the Room and the estate write, not the thing the product is built around**:
a transmission ledger — match history with provenance attached. It records *events*, never
attainment: *Received* (a rhythm genuinely transmitted, including via a paid private lesson —
R4's carve-out), the optional *Heard* tier if ever adopted (a master listened and responded
publicly, by name — recorded as available, not load-bearing), and *Confirmed* (he named it
yours). Confirmed stays exactly where it was: allocated by Junior alone (A5 — RULED),
non-purchasable, the apex the Room can make more *likely* without ever entitling.

Invariants, enforced in the API contract layer (§3.5):

- **A Confirmed entry carries no link to any payment record** (R4). G0's audit becomes a query.
- **No user-claimed entries, ever.** Entries are system-generated from actual events;
  confirmations flow only through the platform channel.
- **Tradition-governed visibility.** Junior's partition governs what is visible, item by item;
  the register cannot be complete and does not pretend to be.
- **Withdrawal-proof.** An entry survives the disappearance of the material it points at
  (red line #6: material comes down, credit is permanent).
- **Record from day one, present later.** Lineage cannot be backfilled: Release 1 carries the
  ledger and shows the first entry; the full presentation surface waits for Release 3.

### 3.5 · The sacred partition (R20 — ADOPTED, five values; enforcement language corrected — D17)

A per-catalog-item field, **set by Junior**:

*playable-inside · gamifiable (recognition/vocalization only) · teachable-but-not-gamifiable ·
archive-only · unrecordable*

**Where it is enforced, stated accurately.** v1.0 said "enforced at the data layer." The audit
found that ACF already supports a five-value single-select per item and the dormant catalog code
defines fields of exactly that shape — but WordPress post meta is schemaless and enforces nothing
at the database level. The accurate statement, and the one this plan now uses: **enforced at the
API contract layer, validated on read, with no client-side override path — which holds so long as
every read path goes through the API.** That is a real protection and a design constraint on every
future surface; it is not a database guarantee, and nobody should later rely on wording that
implies one.

"Playable-inside" is added (per Session 03 §3) because a student occupying an ensemble role inside
a recorded liturgical battery is a stronger form question than a recognition quiz. Values are
captured item by item **at the M0 shoot** (INPUT-21) — the rulings can come later, the shoot's
item-by-item context cannot.

**A second use for the same field (R46).** Offline availability, when it ships, is made a function
of the partition value: sacred-side items stream only, secular items may be downloaded. That ties
protection to the actual harm — Junior's material in a nightclub edit, a red-line-#1 problem
rather than a revenue one — instead of to revenue, and reuses a field that already exists.

**The governance fork (open — INPUT-20).** Red line #1 requires that no game mechanics be placed
on sacred material unless its authority designs the form himself. Reading A: the rhythm as a
musical object sits outside red line #1 (the founder designs the form) while the liturgical layer
around it stays inside (orixá association, ceremonial function, ceremony-only variants, songs and
language, consecration status of the drums — Junior designs a form or excludes, item by item).
Reading B: the item stays inside entirely and Junior designs the form. **The plan assumes Reading
A pending Junior's assent**; the practical difference is whether the game's form is blocked on his
creative bandwidth or only on his partition rulings — a game fed by the whole catalog versus part
of it. The standing partition either way: **gamify the secular, archive the sacred**, boundary set
item by item — and its mitigation of risk #2 must be structural: Junior's first-person authorship
visible on the material, his name on the partition, in his voice.

### 4.0 · Entitlement: R13 is already true, in the wrong shape (VERIFIED-INTERNAL 2026-07-30)

**Entitlement architecture (R13 — ADOPTED).** The app asks the backend what an account owns and
never reads a local store receipt; the rails become interchangeable. **The Room is an instrument,
not a catalog (R29 — ADOPTED): it plays whatever the account owns.** This resolves the free/paid
boundary structurally: the free tier owns a small fixed permanent set (red line #5 makes the set a
one-way door — drawn small deliberately, list is the founder's, INPUT-27); course buyers own their
course's material outright (the library stays course-line property); members buy the **cycle** —
the monthly call, the right to submit, the public response. The recurring line sells no content
(R3 holds); the "is a game content?" question dissolves.

**What the audit found, and it is good news.** `GET /wp-json/opanije-mobile/v1/entitlements` is
deployed and live, answering HTTP 503 `service_disabled`. The app reads no local store receipt
anywhere — there is no IAP integration at all, and the purchase adapter implements only
`external-browser` handoff. R13's architecture, which v1.0 expected to build, **already exists in
the codebase and is switched off by a constant** (ledger row 28).

**The shape is wrong in a way that will not survive Release 1.** The entitlement ledger is capped
at **two purchasable courses and one purchase shape**; a third course is described in the code as
a decision, not a config edit. §4.1 requires simultaneously a permanent free set, an arbitrary
number of purchased course lines, and — at M3 — a subscription that exists nowhere in the estate.
**Rewrite the rail now, while it has zero rows and zero users.** It is the cheapest it will ever
be, and it is the one subsystem where the plan's architecture and the code's architecture already
agree in principle.

**Related gaps that become Release 1 scope:**

- **There are no accounts in the app**, and self-registration is **disabled** on both WordPress
  installs. The free door requires in-app account creation — new work.
- Sign-in is designed as **Google OIDC only**. Apple's login-services guideline is likely to
  require an equivalent privacy-preserving option alongside it on iOS (PM-ASSERTION; §19).
- **In-app account deletion** is a store requirement for apps that create accounts, and no
  self-serve deletion path exists in any surface. It interacts with LGPD and with red line #6
  (INPUT-46).
- **Two WordPress installs with two user tables** are the structural cause of "there is no single
  identity." Linking code does not fix it as well as merging would.

### 4.7 · Instrumentation, in plain terms (R50)

**What it is.** The app writing down what happened inside it: this account started a session, played
6 loops, reached level 2, came back on day 7. Nothing more mysterious than that.

**Why it is Release 1 scope and not optional.** M1's exit evidence *is* those numbers —
first-session completion, day-7 return, minutes per active user. Without instrumentation the
milestone cannot exit, because the evidence does not exist to be recorded. A milestone whose exit
evidence cannot be produced is not a milestone.

**The scoping insight that makes it cheap.** §3.3 already requires the app to count minutes, loops
and levels as Layer-1 facts, and the Caderno already requires those events to be recorded
server-side. **The activation metrics are a byproduct of Layer 1, not a second system.** The
recommendation follows:

- **First-party events into our own backend**, reusing the Layer-1 write path. No third-party
  analytics SDK at Release 1 — which keeps the LGPD story simple (no new processor, no new
  disclosure, no data leaving the estate) and avoids adding a vendor to a privacy policy being
  rewritten from scratch anyway.
- **Plus a crash reporter**, which is a different thing and genuinely needed: without it, a crash on
  a device you do not own is invisible.
- Whatever is collected must match what the store declarations say is collected (§4.8).

## 11 · Defensibility and the IP program

### 11.1 · The moat, stated at its real strength — and vindicated by the audit

**A catalog-and-provenance moat, not a network effect and not a register:** the archive itself, the
consent-and-credit architecture, Junior's partition, the stem library, the pedagogical sequence,
the specific masters at the apex, the mark, and the expressive content (the recorded calls, the
audiovisual identity). Protected by **contract, exclusivity and discipline**, not by registration.
The FF1 test: the named clone can record percussionists; he cannot obtain *these recordings under
this rights architecture*, and he has no reason to build the discipline.

**The audit strengthens this framing rather than damaging it.** The most alarming technical finding
— that the code has no human author (§11.6) — lands nowhere near the asset the company sells,
precisely because §11.1 never located the moat in the code. The recordings are human performances
by named humans and carry authors' and neighbouring rights normally.

**The threat model, corrected.** FF1's clone does not steal files; he books a studio and records his
own battery in an afternoon, with better contacts. Stolen stems have little market — they are worth
little without the rights architecture, the partition, the pedagogy and the masters' names, and
nobody can sell them openly. **The realistic loss is a hobbyist making a remix and posting it —
which is a red-line-#1 problem, not a revenue problem.** Junior's material in a nightclub edit is
the harm scenario, and that reframing raises the protection priority slightly rather than lowering
it, and points it at the partition rather than at DRM (R46).

**The pipeline as a cost/speed advantage is now substantially evidenced** (VERIFIED-INTERNAL
2026-07-30): 14 CI workflows, 175 test files across three subsystems, 31 merges to the site
repository in the preceding 24 hours. Ledger row 5 upgrades from REPORTED. The cost log is still
owed.

The counter-positioning candidate remains HYPOTHESIS with its tests (willingness to pay, retention,
referral; master preference over disintermediation) — the moat must be earned in evidence, not
declared.

### 11.2 · What cannot be owned (VERIFIED-EXTERNAL, 2026-07-29 — not legal advice)

**Game mechanics are categorically unpatentable in Brazil**: LPI art. 10 excludes rules of games
(VII), computer programs as such (V), educational methods and schemes (III), and presentation of
information (VI) — a categorical exclusion, not a threshold. No degree of solidity or uniqueness in
a mechanic makes it registrable. US practice (Compendium §910, the *Baker v. Selden* line) likewise
treats rules, scoring and methods of play as uncopyrightable; what is protectable is code, artwork,
audio, text. **A strategy locating uniqueness in the mechanic builds the moat on the only thing
that cannot be owned anywhere.** Uniqueness is not worthless — it is misfiled: it buys market
distinctiveness and conjunto-imagem strength, not exclusivity. A14's "unique and registered" is
therefore delivered as a **portfolio** (§11.4) plus freedom-to-operate (§11.5).

### 11.3 · The name (FF4 — the finding that matters most)

"Opanijé" is the *toque* of Obaluaiê/Omolu. LPI art. 124, III bars registration of signs offending
religious worship; INPI's *Manual de Marcas* §5.08 tests both the sign in itself and **the
connotation it acquires from the goods it designates** — a liturgical rhythm name designating an
*archive* reads differently from the same name designating a *game*. Three independent exposures
converge: examination risk under the connotation test; **red line #1 — the name is sacred material,
assigned to Junior, and nobody has asked him whether marking it is acceptable** (INPUT-32, prior to
and independent of legality); and risk #2 — "we trademarked the name of Omolu's toque" is a
screenshot-shaped sentence. Sequencing (RULED by this plan): **R35 before R34 before any filing or
further public use** — Junior's ruling, then clearance, with the house-mark/product-mark separation
(R43) prepared so either a refusal or an objection is a packaging decision rather than a crisis.

### 11.4 · What is registrable — the portfolio (VERIFIED-EXTERNAL, 2026-07-29)

1. **Marca (INPI/LPI)** — blocked pending §11.3; likely classes 9, 41, 42 (+ retail if the store is
   marked); counsel sets the basket (INPUT-31).
2. **Direito autoral (Lei 9.610/98)** — arises on creation; where the real asset lives: master
   recordings and phonograms, calls, stems, audiovisual identity, written rules as text. Deposit at
   the Biblioteca Nacional is declaratory and produces dated evidence of authorship (R38).
   **Unaffected by §11.6's authorship finding** — these are human performances by named humans.
3. **Registro de Programa de Computador (IN/INPI/PR 099/2019)** — declaratory, cheap, evidences
   authorship of the code's expression (R38). **Conditional on INPUT-37**: where the code has no
   human author, what is filed, by whom, and is it registrable at all?
4. **Desenho industrial (LPI art. 95 ff.) — the most under-used instrument available**: INPI's
   manual expressly admits an application's GUI, static or **dynamic** — the closest instrument in
   existence to protecting the appearance of the mechanic in motion. Novelty dies on public
   disclosure: **file before the screens are public** (R37); the grace period's terms are a counsel
   line (INPUT-35), not relied on. **Unaffected by the authorship finding** — this protects
   appearance, not code.
5. **Marco Legal dos Games (Lei 14.852/2024)** — the electronic-game registration exists in law
   (LPI art. 2, VI), is not yet regulated (INPI rito scheduled 1st half 2027; Portaria MinC
   260/2026 working group). Keep a complete dated asset dossier ready to be an early filer; ask
   counsel whether an audio-first product meets art. 5, I's real-time-image definition (INPUT-33).
6. **Conjunto-imagem / trade dress — the real anti-clone remedy**: no registration; judicial
   protection via unfair competition (LPI arts. 195, 209), turning on distinctiveness plus
   likelihood of confusion, with expert evidence typically required. **Operational consequence: a
   consistent, documented, distinctive audiovisual identity is itself the legal asset** — design
   consistency from Release 1 and a dated design record are legal work as much as brand work (R40).
7. **Patents — considered and declined** for this company at this treasury.
