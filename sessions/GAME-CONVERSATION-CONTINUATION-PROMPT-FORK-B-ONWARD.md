## 3 · Where things stand

**Decided and not up for renegotiation:**

- The app opens rooms; it never moves the student into one (**D52** — Fork A closed).
- Vocalization replaces Western notation product-wide. No tab, no staff, no rhythm grid (**D53**).
- Junior's solfejo carries **both rhythm and stroke**, as do most masters' (**FOUNDER-FACT**, ledger
  row 40). This is what makes the substitution complete rather than lossy.
- Every room is entered voice → voice-over-battery → battery (**D54**).
- Tap-along ships in Release 1 as the door mechanic; the app measures the tap, never the voice
  (**D55**).
- Advanced mode ships: the screen drum, landscape, phone flat, two hands, zones named by the
  syllables (**D56**).

**The engagement toolkit is open** — streaks, targets, XP, celebration, loss aversion,

ten-item list in §10 (plus three additions in Game Addendum 01 §11) are what actually binds.

**The line that replaces the old prohibitions:** *the app never grades your drumming; it may
celebrate everything else.*

### Fork B — Where the first session ends *(work this first)*

**Why it changed.** The original framing asked whether a first session should open with the game or
with the room. That question is dissolved: under D54 every session now opens with a **voice**. The
live question is how far a first session travels.

**Why it matters more than any other fork.** First-session completion is one of M1's two headline
exit-evidence numbers, and FF3's acceptance test is explicit — the two groups a base product must
serve are single-player adults and **beginners who cannot play to a metronome, which is most
beginners**, and *both must be able to play on day one*.

**The four surfaces now available, in order:** voice → tap-along → the drum room → the screen drum.

**The options:**

1. **The session ends at the tap door.** The drum room is session two. Day one is the syllables and
   the tapping, complete and self-contained, closing on the invitation to bring an instrument.
2. **The session ends in the drum room, with no dropout.** The full arc to playing inside the
   master's battery, with Hold & Rejoin's perturbation deferred to a later session.
3. **The session ends on one short dropout at the peak.** The full arc including the central
   mechanic, resolved clean.

**What to weigh:** option 1 arguably breaches FF3 for the person who already has sticks in hand;
option 3 puts the hardest thing in front of the least prepared person on the day the headline number
is measured; option 2 is the safe middle and tests the central bet last, which may mean testing it
never. E19's instrument question already routes people and may make this a two-answer fork rather
than a one-answer fork.

**Touches:** E4, E19, R70, ledger rows 41 and 42.

### Fork C — Does the break/turnaround variant get shot at M0

**The question.** Percussion traditions commonly carry breaks and turnarounds — the lead drum
signals and everyone changes together. Learning to hear the signal coming is a real skill,
practiceable alone, and the app never has to grade it: the break happens and you know whether you
caught it.

**Why it has a deadline.** A break must be *performed*, layered across every part at every speed,
because Junior plays every drum himself (D38/S6). It cannot be rendered from existing stems. It is
roughly **twelve additional passes per rhythm on top of twelve — it doubles his day** — and it is
unrecoverable once the shoot ends.

**Contrast, for calibration:** everything else the game needs is either free at render time
(dropouts, density) or cheap (solfejo at ~8 short passes, samples at 20–40 minutes). This is the one
expensive irrecoverable item.

**The prior question is free:** **INPUT-58** — do these toques carry breaks at all? Ask before
costing anything.

**Options previously sketched:** don't shoot it; shoot it for the free rhythm only; shoot it across
the catalog. Note that the free rhythm is permanent under E13, so whatever is rendered from it is
committed forever.

### Fork D — What celebration is built on, and how the count-in survives wear

**The question.** R60(b) chose the master's count-in as the celebration object, and the evidence
supports the *class* of reward strongly — Deci 1999 finds positive verbal feedback from a valued
source is the one reward type that enhances rather than undermines intrinsic motivation. But a
count-in fired on every advance becomes a jingle inside a week, and Mandate 01 §4.4 forbids
generating a synthetic substitute, so the wear is unrecoverable.

**Partly addressed already:** R72 recommends shooting a bank of count-in and spoken-moment variants,
which is available only at the shoot. That mitigates but does not decide.

**The open part:** how often his voice fires, what carries the moments in between (non-vocal sound
design is unconstrained), and whether the biggest moments — a first Caderno entry, a rhythm finished
— get a different and rarer asset.

**Touches:** INPUT-62 (form assent), R68, R72, E9/D46.

### Fork E — Where advanced mode sits, and whether it is free

**The question.** The screen drum is the most engaging thing in the product and the most likely to
eat it. Risk #23 is the honest version: a successful phone game whose users never buy an instrument,
never book a class and never go to Salvador inverts the funnel the company is built on.

**R70 recommends it ships *behind* the basic door** — voice, then tap, then drum, then screen drum
for those who want more — never as a shortcut past the instrument. That is a recommendation, not a
ruling.

**The free-tier half is permanent.** Under red line #5 and E13, anything given to the free tier
stays, unchanged, forever — including mechanics. Whatever advanced mode is on the free side of the
door is a permanent commitment.

**Blocked on:** **INPUT-69** — Junior's form assent on a glass surface standing in for a drum. This
is the sharpest red-line-#1 question in the estate and it must be shown as a working mockup, never
described.

### Fork F — Sharing and export

**The question.** Advanced mode produces a shareable object as a byproduct: a person vocalizing a
Ketu toque in the master's own syllables while playing it on a phone. That is the only genuine
spread lever in the product and it costs nothing to build.

**And it is a rights problem before it is a growth lever.** A video of someone playing along
contains Junior's audio, distributed to a platform where **red line #6 cannot reach it**. A takedown
obligation that stops at the CDN is not honoured by a service that never heard of us.

**Registered as INPUT-71** — founder and counsel together. **R69 holds no share or export in Release
1 until it is answered.** This is the largest unpriced item in the game design.

### Fork G — Comparison between students

**The question.** Leaderboards and leagues are the one part of the engagement toolkit that both
works well and sits closest to red line #2 — a public rank over a master's material reads as
standing whether or not it is standing.

**Why it is live now rather than theoretical.** Before the screen drum there was nothing to rank.
Tap accuracy and screen-drum accuracy are precise, comparable numbers, which makes leaderboards
genuinely feasible and genuinely tempting for the first time.

**Registered as INPUT-64**, and Mandate 01 permits it *only if the founder rules it explicitly and
it is visibly cosmetic*. Note also that public absolute ranking reduces perceived competence among
weaker learners, which is the FF3 population.

### Fork H — Does the engagement layer run on the free tier

**The question.** Mandate 01's INPUT-66 recommends yes: the free door is where first-session
completion and day-7 return are measured, and those are the two numbers the whole engagement layer
exists to move.

**Why it needs care rather than a nod.** Red line #5 makes anything given to the free tier permanent
— *including the mechanics*. A streak given free is a streak owed forever. The same is true of
whichever rooms, solfejo arrangements and advanced-mode surfaces sit on the free side.

**Interacts with:** Fork E's free-tier half, Fork B's session-one scope, and E17 (the free rhythm is
Junior's, which concentrates the cultural exposure on the free door).

### Fork I — Does the app ever listen *(deferred, deliberately)*

**Recorded so the reasoning is not lost.** D26 removes the microphone from Release 1 entirely. The
question was raised and worked in this session and the position reached was: **presence-plus-playback
is the right product and a deaf Release 1 is the right release.**

- *Presence* — the mic knows someone is drumming. A fact, permitted, and it fixes a real hole:
  today the app counts practice even if the phone was left running.
- *Playback* — record the room and let the student hear their take beside the master's. This is the
  tradition's own method, and the student judges. No machine opinion exists.
- *Measured timing driving advancement* — **forbidden.** Mandate 01 §10 item 3.

**Why it was deferred, not refused.** Reversing D26 brings back mic permission, store privacy
disclosures, minors on the critical path, LGPD exposure against a four-year-stale policy, a
filesystem layer the estate does not have, and scope against risk #21. It also removes the
safety-by-construction Mandate 01 §3 relies on: the promise not to grade you is currently enforced
by hardware absence rather than by restraint.

**No shoot dependency.** This can be built into a mockup and decided late. Revisit with M1 data,
against R45/INPUT-49.

## 7 · Working method

**The founder's format.** Read the situation → ask for an OK → present questions with **three
options each, with pros and cons**. Plain language. He is here to conceptualize; technical decisions
go to the build side, and technical findings surface only when they force a product-level fork.

**Epistemic discipline is load-bearing.** Every claim carries a label: RULED, REPORTED, ASSUMPTION,
HYPOTHESIS, MEASURED, FOUNDER-FACT, PM-ASSERTION, VERIFIED-EXTERNAL, VERIFIED-INTERNAL, ADOPTED,
FALSIFIED. **No ledger row upgrades without dated evidence.** Falsified rows are retained with the
work that would make them true named beside them.

**Deltas are proposed; the founder ratifies.** Operator mandates are the exception — those are
founder-issued and operative on issue.

**Open inputs are reserved** to the founder, Junior, or counsel. Never resolve one unilaterally.

**The standing resource rule (S7):** always choose the option that is cheaper in other people's time
and more expensive in Junior's own hours. He is a co-founder. But S7 says spend his hours, not spend
them without asking — every item that costs him a shoot day is an input, not an assumption.

**The founder's operating rule:** one new standing obligation at a time. This is load-bearing in
every sequencing decision, and Release 1 is already carrying more than it was a week ago.

**Two numbers judge every game element:** first-session completion and day-7 return. Argue against
those, not against whether something is fun.

**And the honest caveat to carry into any engagement argument:** the research base narrows the
design space and does not choose the product. Gamification effects are positive on average,
heterogeneous, and often small. Nothing should be built because it worked in a paper.
