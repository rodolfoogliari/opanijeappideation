## 3 · The focus question: grading without negative classification

### 3.1 · The assumptions questioned

The estate's position on evaluation was built from three assumptions, each examined here and each
found overextended:

**Assumption 1 — "any visible measure of play is a verdict on musicianship."** False by
conflation. Four distinct things were collapsed into one banned category: *measurement* (a strike
occurred at time t in zone z — a fact), *feedback* (what that meant, musically — information),
*grading* (how this round went — a summary of play), and *classification* (what this student is —
a claim about a person). Only the fourth is dangerous, and it is the only one the charter actually
forbids: machine claims of readiness, correctness, mastery, standing. Games resolved this decades
ago — **a game grades the run, never the player.** The run ends; you go again; nothing was said
about you. The estate feared classification and, to avoid it, deleted grading, and with grading
went legibility, and with legibility went the game.

**Assumption 2 — "precision is the quantity to grade."** False for this instrument. G9's three
arguments (unknowable device offset, the surface-becomes-a-drum problem, FF3's beginner) are all
arguments against **precision readouts** — and they are correct against precision readouts. They
were then overextended to all post-round visibility. The musical quantity a battery actually cares
about is not millisecond offset; it is **presence** — are you with us, and for how long. Sustain
measured at cycle granularity through a generous window is robust to device latency, meaningful to
a beginner, and frames error as *absence* rather than *wrongness*. That reframe is the entire
emotional difference between a game and a test.

**Assumption 3 — "silence protects the student."** False; silence produced risk #25, the
competence illusion — the student who never knows. The protective move is not less information but
information in the **audible channel**, where a real drum delivers it: the wrong zone sounds
wrong, the off-time strike sounds as itself, the unfed part thins. A real battery never issues a
sentence about you, and it never lets you not know.

### 3.2 · The grading constitution (D76, ADOPTED)

One principle replaces the patchwork of §10 items, G9 carve-outs and per-surface rulings:

> **Facts may drive sound; nothing may drive a sentence.**
> **Sustain is visible, precision is audible, judgment is human.**
> **The game grades the round; the ear grades the playing; the master grades the player.**

The app may operate mechanically on facts and render musical consequences. It may never express an
evaluation in words, numbers, meters, tiers, stars, letters or percentages about the quality of
playing — and it may never say anything at all about the player.

### 3.3 · The four feedback levels

**Level 1 — during play: entirely audible (plus one honest light).**

- **The drum always sounds (D77, ADOPTED; resolves C22).** In-window strikes keep Junior's correct
  part alive — G10 unchanged. **Out-of-window strikes fire a one-shot of the real stroke** — the
  student's actual hit, audible, off the groove — because a real drum struck off-time still
  sounds. Same for wrong-zone strikes (already designed, Addendum 02 §5). This is the information
  a physical instrument gives, with no readout, and it restates D58's justification on solid
  ground: the sample library exists so the drum is honest. Latency is tolerable here precisely
  because an off-grid hit has no exact reference to smear against.
- **The part fades when unfed; it returns when fed (D78, ADOPTED — the specified reading of G10's
  "keeps it alive").** There is **no fail state anywhere in the product.** The battery never stops
  for you; your part thins when you stop feeding it and comes back when you strike again. You
  cannot lose; you can only be more or less present in the music — which is what happens in a real
  battery when a player drops out and rejoins. This resolves the competence illusion more honestly
  than either silence or a fail screen.
- **E21 (PM-RECOMMENDED): the presence lamp.** E2 already lights the named musicians as each
  joins. The student's own slot glows the same way while their part is fed and dims as it fades —
  a one-to-one visual mirror of the audio, no numbers, consistent with §3.2. The student joins the
  named musicians on screen, which is also the product's promise made visible.
- Nothing else appears mid-play. E3 stands: propped phone, arm's length, no menus.
- **The voice is never measured, in any mode.** Unchanged, absolute.

**Level 2 — after the round: the game grades the round (D79, ADOPTED; amends G9's scope).**

Only after the musical ending (E9's quiet count, upgraded):

- **Three facts of the run:** cycles the battery played with you, longest unbroken hold, and the
  setting (part, drums present, tempo, mode). A *fed cycle* is one where a generous proportion of
  the part's strikes landed in the generous window — cycle granularity, never milliseconds.
- **One personal best: longest hold at this setting.** Self-referenced (you against your own
  yesterday), monotonic (it only ever goes up), per-setting (a best at more exposure is a
  different best). **When it is beaten, that is the celebration moment** — INPUT-73 is answered:
  the celebration object is the new personal best, carried by non-vocal sound design and the
  room's light (D80, ADOPTED). Junior's voice variants stay reserved for firsts and returns —
  first entry, first rhythm finished, return after absence (R66's repair asset) — and the count-in
  remains the opening ritual (G16 untouched).
- **The monotonic display rule (R84):** the screen never says "below your best." Short of the
  best, only the neutral facts show. Equal or beyond, the best updates and the room celebrates.
  A number that only ever rises cannot humiliate.
- **What the amendment to G9 is, exactly:** per-strike feedback remains binary, generous and
  audible-only; what changes is that a **post-round aggregate of those binaries** (sustain)
  becomes visible. Accuracy-style readouts remain barred on every surface. G9's device-fairness
  argument does not reach cycle-granular sustain through a generous window; its FF3 argument is
  answered by self-reference and monotonic display; its surface argument is answered by the
  sentence ban. Flagged plainly as the one place this file amends a founder ruling's scope; put to
  the founder separately and **ratified 2026-08-03 (RULED)**.

**Level 3 — across rounds: the map lights, never dims (D75 + R65 carried).**

The repertoire map (§4.2) records rooms visited, rhythms met, parts carried, personal bests. All
monotonic. The play ledger stays resettable by design — the two-ledger architecture was built for
exactly this and is finally load-bearing. Doors open on facts (D52 untouched): the app opens,
never promotes, never asserts readiness. **The vocabulary rule (R86):** play-layer words are
spatial and musical — rooms, visits, holds, bests — and never borrow the Caderno's words. No
"received," "confirmed," "mastered," "level," "rank," "graduate." The wording is where
classification would leak back in, so the wording is specified.

**Level 4 — the only place the person is ever assessed: the master, monthly, by name.**

Layer 2, unchanged, human, capped by his hours — and pulled into Release 1 operationally (§5).
The game deliberately points at it: the ending's invitation (E7/E10) now includes the cycle.

### 3.4 · The frustration audit

Every known frustration source in rhythm-game design, checked against this architecture:

- **Fail states** — none exist; fade-and-rejoin replaces them.
- **Forced repetition** — none; E8's free part-switching in both directions stands, with R58's
  framing (fewer drums is exposure, more drums is shelter; moving down is a move).
- **Difficulty walls** — none; the student sets every axis, and the door's alternative
  (behavioural facts) opens rooms on presence alone.
- **Negative feedback** — none in sentences; error lives in sound, where it informs without
  addressing the person.
- **Comparison** — none (G17 stands).
- **Loss** — nothing is ever taken (red lines #2 and #5; no hearts, no decay, monotonic records).
- **Illegibility** — the one frustration the old design *created* — answered by the echo reveal
  (§4.1), the honest drum, and the visible hold.
- **Boredom** — answered by the echo loop and the repertoire arc (§4).

### 3.5 · What remains barred, restated so the plan is safe

Accuracy percentages, stars, letters, tiers, "perfect/good/miss" labels, fail screens, streaks
with loss, person-levels, readiness or mastery claims, public rank, comparison between students,
purchasable repair, synthetic voice, any game result touching the Caderno, any measurement of the
voice. Unchanged, all of it.

### 3.6 · Why the amendment stays below the charter

D79 amends **G9**, a founder design ruling, not a red line. Red line #2 (standing only earned) is
untouched — sustain confers nothing and writes nowhere. Mandate 01 §3's "no machine verdict on
musicianship" is honoured under §3.1's distinction: a round summary is a fact about play, framed
against the player's own history, expressed without sentences about the person. Whether that
distinction holds *in the student's experience* is an empirical question and is instrumented as
such (ledger row 49; pilot questions, R87).

## 4 · The game, assembled

### 4.1 · The micro-game: the echo loop (D74, ADOPTED; form assent INPUT-79)

The in-room event Release 1 lost (dropouts out under G12, breaks deferred under G24) returns
through the **voice channel**, not the battery — which G12 never touched. Oral pedagogy runs on an
echo shape: the teacher voices the part; the student joins; the teacher's voice withdraws as the
student holds it; teacher and student land together. Rendered:

> **voice together → voice withdraws → you alone (battery continues) → voice returns → you hear
> whether you held.**

The battery is never artificially perturbed; the teaching voice receding is not a game mechanic
but the method itself — which G20 has just confirmed is the tradition's. Every state is
render-time muting on solfejo recorded locked to the battery (R63), so the loop is nearly free.
Two conditions before it is settled: **INPUT-78** — the classroom transcription: how a real lesson
with Junior actually runs, minute by minute, because the proven classroom (G21) is the loop's
specification and the estate has never asked for it; and **INPUT-79** — Junior's form assent that
the rendered withdrawal is his teaching and not a trick played on it.

### 4.2 · The macro-game: the repertoire arc (D75, ADOPTED)

Under G23 the catalog is wide, and under G25 its commons layer is free. The map therefore stops
being a difficulty grid for one rhythm and becomes **the city**: Ijexá, afoxé, samba de roda,
samba-reggae, the toques Junior is authorised to teach — each a room, each entered voice-first,
each carrying its own personal bests. Progress is *rhythms met and carried further*, day-7 return
gets a next room that is genuinely new, and the free commons rooms are simultaneously the
standard's teaching corpus (§6). R65 stands: the map is the state space, lit — one object.

### 4.3 · The session, end to end

Count-in (G16) → voice together → the echo reveal → the student's chosen branch (more exposure,
more drums, another tempo, another part — E8) → a second reveal or a clean full-battery close →
the quiet count, three facts, the best if beaten → the invitation (E7/E10, now including the
cycle). Battery Detective survives as an optional thirty-second prelude, not the spine.

## 6 · The standard (D82, ADOPTED — workstream opened)

Under G22, publication moves from defensive to strategic. What is published is the **frame**, not
the corpus: the syllable system's structure, presented as a standard for teaching Afro-Brazilian
percussion — never the recordings, stems, or renders, which remain the priced scarcity.
**INPUT-67 reframes rather than closes:** the app fixes **Junior's syllable set as the reference
dialect** for the material he teaches, inside a frame that lets other masters and other traditions
carry their own — which is how living standards for oral material work, and how red line #1 scales
to every future bench without one master's syllables being imposed on another's tradition.
FF1 flips valence: an incumbent adopting a standard Opanijé publishes and stewards strengthens the
position, provided stewardship is designed. **INPUT-83** — founder and counsel: publication scope,
licence, timing relative to the design filing and the existing defensive-publication plan, and the
music-education positioning's effect on the trademark strategy (counsel's question, not asserted
here). **Risk #28** records the race between publication and the reference corpus maturing.

## 14 · Risks (continuing #26)

**27 · Sustain becomes a proxy score.** Players chase the hold the way they would an accuracy
number, and pressure returns through the back door. *Mitigation: nothing mid-play, monotonic
display, no comparison, resettable ledger, and row 49's guilt items measured rather than assumed.*

**28 · The standard outruns the corpus.** Publication invites adoption — including by FF1's
