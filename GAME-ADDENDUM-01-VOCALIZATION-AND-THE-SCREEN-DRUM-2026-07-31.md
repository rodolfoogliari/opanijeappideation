## 2 · The founder fact that makes the substitution safe

**G3 is load-bearing and it is the reason G2 is not a loss.**

Western tablature carries two things: *when* a stroke falls, and *which stroke it is* — open, slap,
bass, muffled, and which hand. A notation that carried only rhythm would be a downgrade dressed as
a cultural choice, and the honest position an hour ago was that we did not know which we had.

We now do. **Junior's solfejo encodes stroke as well as rhythm, and this is general across
masters** (FOUNDER-FACT, treated as true). The syllable is not a rhythmic placeholder; it names the
sound. That makes the substitution complete rather than partial, and it makes three separate things
in this product the same object:

- **The notation** — the syllables are what a part looks like.
- **The teaching** — the syllables are how a part is transmitted, in the tradition's own method.
- **The input** — the syllable tells you which zone to strike in advanced mode (§6).

One system does all three. Nothing in the estate has previously collapsed three problems into one
artifact at zero cost, and this is the strongest structural result of the session.

**What remains open, narrowed.** INPUT-67 no longer asks whether solfejo encodes stroke. It asks
whether the app **fixes one syllable set as its standard** — because whatever we record becomes the
transmission standard for everyone who learns here, permanently. That is a form question under
Mandate 01 §4.3, not a factual one, and it is Junior's.

**A moat consequence, stated without legal claim.** Anyone can transcribe a toque to tab in an
afternoon and publish it. This notation is inseparable from a recording of a specific man that
nobody else can obtain, which makes the non-publication discipline (v2.0 §11 — the catalog, the
partition, the stem architecture, the pedagogical sequence) materially easier to hold. Counsel
should be told this is now the shape of it; nothing here is an IP opinion.

**A cost, stated so it is not discovered later.** No notation means nothing to print, nothing to
study away from the app, nothing to check against a second source. Some adult learners want exactly
that, and competitors will have tabs. This is accepted, not overlooked.

## 3 · Fork A, closed (G1)

**The question.** Mandate 01 §9 and D51 permit the app to advance the student on behavioural facts.
The game's feedback event — the master's part drops out, you continue, it returns, and *you* hear
whether you held — puts the judgment in the student's ear. Those collide: the app can carry someone
into a bigger room on time-served while their own ears have been telling them, every round, that
they are drifting. The app is the only thing in the room that does not know.

**The closure.** D51 stands. Its operative form narrows from *moving* to *opening*.

- **The app opens rooms.** Two things open one: behavioural facts as Mandate 01 §9 already
  describes (time in the room, cycles, sessions returned, days present), or holding the solfejo
  pattern at the door (§5).
- **Neither is required.** E8 is untouched. Any room can be walked into at any time, in either
  direction, and R58's reframe holds — fewer drums is exposure, more drums is shelter, moving down
  is a move rather than a retreat.
- **The app never asserts readiness.** It asserts that a door is open. This is why the collision
  stops existing rather than being managed: there is no claim about the student for his ear to
  contradict.

**What this preserves from the three options considered.** The count-in remains available as the
opening moment, but reserved — a first entry, a rhythm finished — rather than fired on every
advance. See §8.3 on why that reservation is a design requirement and not restraint.

**Recorded as D52.** This narrows D51 and does not withdraw it. INPUT-63 stays closed.

## 4 · Vocalization as the notation (G2)

**The ruling.** No Western notation on any surface: no tablature, no staff, no rhythm grid, no
piano-roll. Vocalization is how a part is written, shown, taught and referenced throughout the
product.

**What this touches.**

- **Subtitling (E11, INPUT-14), partially.** Syllables cross every language. Junior *explaining* in
  Portuguese does not, and narration remains the most subtitle-dense content in the estate. The
  gain is real and bounded: the demonstration goes free, the teaching around it does not.
- **The design brief (R54, D48).** It was already carrying two moods — practice stillness and
  celebration. It now also carries the syllable as a visual object, and under §6 as a spatial one.
  This is on the Release 1 critical path through R49's lock-and-file.
- **Accessibility.** A product with no visual notation has nothing to offer a deaf or
  hard-of-hearing user, and the syllables are the only representation available. Recorded as a
  known limitation, not a blocker.
- **INPUT-36, promoted.** *Vocalization as the tradition's own teaching method* has been a standing
  PM-ASSERTION and a side verification. It is now the foundation this entire direction rests on.
  If it is false, this is a rhythm game with a candomblé skin, which is precisely the object the
  plan has spent three sessions avoiding. **Ask it first, and ask it plainly.**

## 5 · The door: voice, then tap, then drum (G4, G5)

### 5.1 · The sequence

Every room is entered the same way:

1. **Voice alone.** The syllables, no drums. You hear the part as a sentence before you hear it as a
   sound.
2. **Voice over the battery.** The landing moment — the syllables sitting on the actual drum, so
   the sentence and the sound become the same thing.
3. **Battery.** The room as it already exists.

**This is free.** Once the solfejo is recorded locked to the battery (§9.1, R63), all three states
are render-time muting on material already in the can. It is exactly what the subtraction ladder
does, applied to a voice instead of a drum, and it needs no audio engine and no INPUT-41 answer.

### 5.2 · Tap-along, and what it is for

The student vocalizes and taps together. **The app measures the tap and never the voice** — there
is no microphone (D26) and the voice stays unmeasured by construction. This keeps the governance
line clean: a screen tap is a screen fact, not a musical verdict, and it sits on the same side of
Mandate 01 §3 as Battery Detective.

**E3 settles what tap-along is not.** The phone is propped up and the hands hold sticks; nobody
taps and drums at once. Tap-along is therefore **the door, not the session** — what you do before
you have an instrument, on the bus, or in the first ninety seconds before you have committed.
Which is exactly where FF3's beginner lives and exactly where first-session completion is won.

**The risk, named because it is structural.** Tap-along is easy to build, easy to instrument, easy
to celebrate and easy to get good at. Drumming is none of those. Left to itself the screen game
becomes the product, because it is the part that measures well. §6 makes this risk larger, not
smaller, and §10 states the mitigation.

### 5.3 · What the hand does while the mouth does the part

Three real possibilities, and they are different skills:

- **Unison** — tap the rhythm you are voicing. Reinforcing, learnable on day one.
- **Pulse** — tap the pulse while voicing the part. Polyrhythmic independence; much harder; arguably
  the actual drummer's skill.
- **Reference** — tap the agogô while voicing your own part.

**PM-RECOMMENDED: unison first, independence as a later room.** But this is pedagogy and it is
Junior's — INPUT-68.

## 6 · Advanced mode: the screen drum (G6)

### 6.1 · What it is

Phone flat on a table, **landscape**, two hands. The screen is a drum head divided into zones. The
student vocalizes the part and strikes the zone the syllable names.

**Because G3 is true, the syllable *is* the instruction.** Say the bass syllable, strike the bass
zone. Say the slap syllable, strike the slap zone. There is no separate notation to read, no legend
to learn, and no translation step between what you say and what you do. This is the payoff of §2 and
it is the reason advanced mode is a small design rather than a large one.

### 6.2 · What it can and cannot encode

**Can:** which stroke, and when.

**Cannot: how hard.** Touch velocity is unreliable across devices and must not be relied on. This is
an honest limitation and it is also useful — it keeps the screen drum a *transcription* device
rather than a simulation of playing, which is the framing that protects both the governance line
and the real instrument.

**PM-RECOMMENDED: three zones to start** — bass, open, slap — mapped to the three primary syllable
families, two-handed. Do not build five before Junior has seen three.

### 6.3 · Where the new red-line exposure is

**This must not be waved through.** Everything else in this session sits comfortably inside the
existing charter. This does not.

Under Reading A (INPUT-20, assumed pending Junior's assent), the rhythm-as-musical-object sits
outside red line #1 while the liturgical layer stays inside — and §3.5 names **consecration status
of the drums** explicitly as part of that inside layer.

A glass rectangle standing in for a consecrated drum is a form question **about the drum**, not
about the rhythm. It is closer to red line #1 than anything else proposed in three sessions, and it
is a form question whether or not anyone intended it as one — the same test Mandate 01 §4.3 applies
to a confetti burst over a toque of Obaluaiê.

**This is not a veto and it is not expensive.** D36 already puts the mockup before the shoot so
Junior sees a working thing instead of a description. Advanced mode goes into that mockup and he
rules on the form he can actually see. **INPUT-69**, gathered with INPUT-41, INPUT-52 and INPUT-62.

Build it, show it, adjust the form if he asks. But do not ship it unshown.

### 6.4 · What it requires that we do not have

**A stroke sample library.** The screen produces no sound when struck, so the app must. That needs
clean one-shot samples: each drum, each stroke type, several velocity layers, several takes.

**These are not derivable from the battery stems.** You cannot cleanly extract individual strokes
from a performance. It is a separate capture — same room, same instruments, same day — and it is
**irrecoverable afterwards** on exactly the same terms as the stems themselves.

It is also cheap: mechanical, no performance, no take direction. **ASSUMPTION: twenty to forty
minutes of shoot time for the free rhythm's instruments.** See §9.2.

## 7 · The partition tier this activates

**The finding.** The five-value sacred partition (v2.0 §3.5, R20) is:

> *playable-inside · **gamifiable (recognition/vocalization only)** · teachable-but-not-gamifiable ·
> archive-only · unrecordable*

The second value has been close to dead weight. The Room is a playable-inside product; an item
Junior marks *vocalization only* has, until today, had almost nothing to do inside it.

**G2 through G6 build the game that tier was written for.** Vocalization, tap-along and the screen
drum are all recognition-and-vocalization forms. They do not seat a student in an ensemble role
inside a recorded liturgical battery — which §3.5 identifies as the stronger form question and the
reason *playable-inside* was added as a separate value.

**What follows.** Material Junior would never mark playable-inside may now be marked gamifiable and
have a real game to be gamifiable *into* — using a form the partition itself already sanctions,
rather than one we invented. This expands the catalog available to the game without straining
Reading A, and it strengthens the INPUT-20 position rather than leaning on it.

**Operational consequence (R64).** INPUT-21's item-by-item partition capture at M0 should be taken
with this tier live in mind. "Is this gamifiable in vocalization only?" is a different question when
that answer has a product behind it than when it meant *nothing much happens to this item*.

## 8 · The engagement design, against the evidence

*The founder's instruction was to use the research to make this a phenomenon. §8.4 states honestly
what the research can and cannot deliver against that word.*

### 8.1 · What the evidence base actually supports

From the two research documents in the estate, ranked by evidence strength, and constrained to what
Mandate 01 permits:

| Lever | Grade | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| Intrinsic reward of groove/entrainment | STRONG | The activity supplies its own pull. A language app must manufacture reward; this one is on home ground |
| Need support — autonomy, competence, relatedness | STRONG | Ryan/Rigby/Przybylski 2006; the spine |
| **Positive verbal feedback from a valued source** | **STRONG (meta-analytic)** | Deci 1999: tangible performance-contingent rewards *undermine* intrinsic motivation (d ≈ −0.28 to −0.40); positive verbal feedback *enhances* it (d ≈ +0.33). **This is the single most actionable result in the base** |
| Implementation intentions / cue design | STRONG (d = 0.65) | Highest evidence-to-cost ratio available. Near-zero build |
| Peak–end session shape | STRONG on evaluation | Alaybek 2022 (r = 0.58); Redelmeier 2003 on actual return. Effect on D7 remains HYPOTHESIS |
| Streaks | MODERATE | Silverman & Barasch 2023; Mehr 2025. Real persistence lever, real confound |
| Self-monitoring / pure counting | MODERATE | Harkin 2016 (d ≈ 0.40 *with* goals; unquantified without) |
| Curiosity / epistemic discovery | MODERATE | Gruber & Ranganath 2019: gaps that feel *resolvable* |

**And the general caution that governs all of it.** Gamification effects are positive on average,
heterogeneous, and often small — Sailer & Homner's g ≈ 0.25–0.49, with only the cognitive effect
robust under high methodological rigour; Li et al. 2024's g = 0.257 on intrinsic motivation with
minimal effect on competence. **Nothing here should be copied because it worked in a paper.** The
research narrows the design space; the product still has to be measured.

### 8.2 · R60's three mechanics, specified

Mandate 01 R60 already chose three. This session specifies them rather than reopening them.

**(a) The daily-return streak.** Ships. Two design requirements from the evidence:

- **Repair must exist and must not be purchasable.** Silverman & Barasch find repair attenuates the
  break effect; Mandate 01 §4.1 forbids anything purchasable that touches standing, and §4.2
  forbids anything that gates access. **R66: repair is musical.** Junior's recorded voice on return
  — a welcome back, not a reproach, not a currency, not a shop.
- **Measure return *after* the first break, not only D7 while intact.** A streak that has never been
  tested has not been measured.

**(b) Celebration built on the master's count-in.** Ships, reserved. See §8.3.

**(c) The room map showing what is lit.** Ships — and **R65: the map IS the difficulty state space,
lit. Do not build two objects.** The rooms are already a grid: whether your own part is with you,
how many drums are with you, how fast, and now whether you are in voice, tap, drum or screen-drum.
That grid rendered as "what is lit" is simultaneously the map, the navigation and the record of
where you have been. R61 permits fractions and totals for rooms; the addendum's "12 of 36 is the
exact failure mode" is withdrawn for rooms and **retained absolutely for the Caderno**.

**One addition, on evidence strength alone.** **R67: an implementation intention at onboarding** —
when and where you will practise. Gollwitzer & Sheeran's d = 0.65 is the largest well-supported
effect in the entire research base and it costs one screen. It also gives D45's notification
permission a natural and honest moment: you asked to be called back at a time you chose.

### 8.3 · The reward vocabulary

**R68: the reward vocabulary is Junior's real voice plus non-vocal sound design. Nothing else.**

This is not aesthetics. Deci 1999 is the one meta-analytic result that tells you *which class* of
reward to spend: tangible, expected, performance-contingent rewards undermine free-choice intrinsic
motivation; **positive informational feedback from a valued source enhances it.** Junior's voice is
simultaneously the reward class the evidence endorses, the asset no competitor can obtain, and the
thing R60(b) already selected. Three arguments converging on one object is rare and should be spent
deliberately.

**Which is why it must be rationed.** A count-in fired on every advance stops being a musical event
within a week and becomes a jingle — and §4.4 forbids generating a synthetic substitute, so wear-out
is unrecoverable. Fork D remains open on exactly this. The pre-shoot mitigation is cheap and
available only once: **shoot a bank of variants** — several count-ins, several calls, a handful of
spoken moments — and turn wear into variety. See §9.3.

### 8.4 · On "phenomenon", stated honestly

**No peer-reviewed evidence tells you how to make a phenomenon.** The base above is retention
evidence. Spread is a different problem and the literature does not address it. Anyone who claims
otherwise is selling folklore, and the estate's discipline is to say so.

What can be said with a straight face:

- **The artifact is genuinely novel.** A person vocalizing a Ketu toque in the master's own
  syllables while playing it on a phone in landscape is a thing that does not currently exist. That
  is a *necessary* condition for spread and not a sufficient one.
- **It is inherently filmable.** Advanced mode is a performance — a body doing something visible and
  audible. This is the only mechanic in the product that produces a shareable object as a byproduct
  rather than requiring one to be manufactured.
- **And that is a rights problem before it is a growth lever.** A video of someone playing along
  contains Junior's audio, distributed outside the app, where **red line #6 cannot reach it.** A
  takedown obligation that stops at the CDN is not honoured by a platform that never heard of us.
  **INPUT-71** — founder and counsel, together. **R69: no share or export in Release 1 until it is
  answered.** This is the single largest unpriced item created by this session.

### 8.5 · Novelty decay

Gamification effects commonly decline from around week four (Rodrigues 2022's U-shape). Designs
anchored in intrinsic and pedagogical need-satisfaction overcame that drop-off where purely
extrinsic ones did not (Tsay 2020).

**Consequence for measurement:** D7 is not enough. **Measure D30 as a matter of course**, and expect
the streak's contribution to be at its maximum exactly when the first numbers are taken. Ledger row
43 records this so the first cohort is not read as proof.

### 8.6 · Telling the game from the streak, inside one cohort

Ledger row 38 already says Release 1 ships once and there is no counterfactual. So arms are not
available and D50's per-mechanic instrumentation has to do the work. **One measure does most of it:**

> **A streak is satisfied by the minimum session. The game is not.**

So instrument what happens **after the streak has already ticked** — rounds played past the
threshold, replays of rooms already completed, session length once the counter is secure. Add the
users who decline notifications as a free, self-selected quasi-control. That is a defensible read of
whether the musical loop is pulling, with no arms and no sample-size problem.

**R71.** For completeness: the randomised design proposed in the game-mechanics research review is
not available at Release 1 on two counts — row 38 forecloses it, and detecting a ten-point D7
difference would need roughly 291 users per arm (582 total), with about 2,500 needed for five
points, while splitting the cohort halves precision on both of M1's headline numbers.

## 9 · The M0 shoot, as it now stands

*Everything in this section is unrecoverable after the cameras stop.*

### 9.1 · Solfejo passes

**The architectural decision, and it is the whole scope in one line: record the solfejo with the
battery in his headphones, locked to the grid, never standalone (R63).**

Locked, you can solo it later and get the standalone for free. Standalone, you can never lock it
afterwards. One direction is reversible and the other is not — the same logic that put the stems at
M0 rank 3.

Baseline for the free rhythm is four parts and three speeds ≈ twelve drum passes (Session-2
addendum §5).

| Scope | Solfejo passes | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Every part, every speed | 12 | Complete; nothing to regret |
| **Every part, slow + normal** | **8** | **PM-RECOMMENDED** — the top speed is where nobody is on the on-ramp |
| Every part, one reference speed | 4 | Cheapest; locks the door to one tempo permanently |

**ASSUMPTION on time:** solfejo passes are short and low-setup — he is already miked, the battery is
already in his ears, and a pass needs enough cycles to learn rather than three minutes. Roughly a
fifth to a quarter of a drum pass in wall-clock time. Eight passes therefore costs something like
two drum passes' worth of the day — **a fifth added, not a doubling.**

Tempo choice folds into **INPUT-59**, which is already pre-shoot because the count-ins are
tempo-locked. Same lock, same reason: a solfejo is stuck at whatever speed it was spoken at.

### 9.2 · The stroke sample library (new)

Required by §6.4. Not derivable from the stems. Not recoverable later.

| Item | Scope |
|---|---|
| Drums | Every instrument in the free rhythm's battery |
| Strokes | Every stroke the solfejo names — which under G3 is exactly the set that matters |
| Layers | Several velocity layers per stroke, several takes each |
| Format | Isolated one-shots, clean, no room bleed from other instruments |

**INPUT-70** — Junior: which strokes, and whether any stroke or drum is off-limits to sampling under
the partition. A one-shot of a consecrated drum is a different object from a passage of music and
should be asked about as one.

**ASSUMPTION on time:** twenty to forty minutes. Mechanical, no performance direction.

## 10 · What this costs

Stated so it is not discovered later, in the manner of Mandate 01 §5.

- **Release 1 build scope grows again.** A tap engine, a screen-drum surface with sample playback,
  and the voice-first entry sequence, on top of the play-layer subsystem, notifications and
  celebration assets Mandate 01 added, on top of D37's store billing which was new this week.
  Against risk #21 — *the failure mode is not a bad Release 1; it is no Release 1* — this is now the
  thing to watch, and the founder's rule of one new standing obligation at a time is under real
  pressure.
- **The design brief grows a third time (D48).** Practice stillness, celebration, and now the
  syllable as both a typographic and a spatial object, in two orientations. Release 1 critical path
  through R49.
- **Latency is coupled to the design, not to the platform.** Touch and audio latency vary widely
  across Android. Grade tightly and per-device calibration becomes a project; ask only whether the
  student was on the pulse, with a generous window, and device variation stops mattering. This is
  build-side, but it is the decision that makes advanced mode cheap or expensive and it should be
  made early.
- **INPUT-39 grows again.** A screen-drum game aimed at a population that includes minors, with
  notifications and engagement telemetry, is more compliance surface, not less.
- **The share question is unpriced** (§8.4, INPUT-71) and is the largest open item created here.
